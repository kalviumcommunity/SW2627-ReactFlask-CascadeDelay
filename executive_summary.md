# CHURN REDUCTION INITIATIVE

## Executive Summary

### Situation

Customer churn is our leading revenue loss driver, costing approximately $2M annually. Our current churn rate is 7%, compared with an industry average of 4%. The analysis identified support response time as a major opportunity to improve retention and protect revenue.

### Key Findings

- **Support speed directly impacts retention:** Customers receiving support within 2 hours churn at 3%, compared with 12% for customers waiting more than 24 hours — a 4x difference.
- **Response time is a major bottleneck:** The support team currently averages a 6-hour response time, significantly above the less-than-2-hour target.
- **High-value customers are particularly vulnerable:** Customers spending more than $10K annually churn at 15% when support is slow, making this segment especially important to protect.

### Business Risks

- **Revenue loss:** At the current 7% churn rate, approximately $2M in annual revenue is at risk.
- **High-value customer loss:** Losing major customers creates disproportionate financial impact and threatens valuable relationships.
- **Competitive vulnerability:** Competitors with stronger support experiences can attract dissatisfied customers.
- **Operational pressure:** Support ticket volume has increased 40% year over year while response times have deteriorated.

### Recommendations

1. **Hire 2 Support Engineers — $200K/year:** Reduce average response time from 6 hours to below 2 hours, with an expected $400K reduction in churn-related losses and a projected 2x first-year ROI.
2. **Implement a <2-hour Response SLA — $0:** Establish a clear response-time target, track performance daily, and create accountability. Implementation begins January 1.
3. **Prioritize High-Value Customers — $50K engineering:** Create a dedicated support lane for customers spending more than $10K annually, with a target of reducing high-value churn by 50%. Implementation begins February 1.

### Decision Needed

Approve the $200K annual hiring budget and $50K engineering investment by December 15, with a projected $400K reduction in churn-related losses during the first year.

### Next Steps

Operations reviews the hiring and SLA plan by December 15. Finance approves the budget by December 20. Hiring begins January 1, with new hires expected to become productive by April 1.
